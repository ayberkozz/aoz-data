# AI Papers - 2026-10-04

## Short read (~15 min): Distilling the Knowledge in a Neural Network
- Authors / affiliation: Geoffrey Hinton, Oriol Vinyals, Jeff Dean (Google)
- Why credible: Hinton is a Turing Award winner and one of the "godfathers" of modern AI; Dean leads much of Google's AI work.
- Link: [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.02531) | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.02531)
- Topic: Small / on-device models
- What this paper is about: Big AI models are accurate but slow and too heavy to run on a phone. This paper shows a trick called "distillation": you let a big, smart "teacher" model answer lots of examples, then train a much smaller "student" model to copy the teacher's answers. The key idea is that the teacher's answers carry extra hints. For example, it says "this photo is 90% cat, 9% dog, 1% car", and the "9% dog" tells the student that cats look a bit like dogs. After reading, you will understand how big models get shrunk into small ones, which is how many on-device AI features can run fast on a phone.

## Long read (~2-3 hours): A Survey on Evaluation of Large Language Models
- Authors / affiliation: Yupeng Chang, Xu Wang, Jindong Wang, and others, including Philip S. Yu and Qiang Yang (Jilin University, Microsoft Research Asia, University of Illinois Chicago, HKUST and others)
- Why credible: Co-authors are very well-known professors and Microsoft Research scientists, and it was accepted by the journal ACM Transactions on Intelligent Systems and Technology.
- Link: [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03109) | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.03109) (45 pages)
- Topic: Evaluation
- What this paper is about: How do you know if an AI chatbot is actually good? This survey is a big map of how people test large language models (LLMs, the AI behind chatbots). It is organized around three questions: what to test (reasoning, math, medicine, ethics, safety and more), where to test (the standard test sets, called benchmarks), and how to test (automatic scoring versus human judges). It also points out where today's models still fail. After reading, you will be able to understand claims like "this model scored 85% on a benchmark" and judge whether they mean much.
- Suggested sections to start with: the Introduction, then the section on "What to evaluate" (skim the task categories), then "How to evaluate". Treat the benchmark tables as a reference and skip the detailed per-domain sections at first.
